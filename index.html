03 · Insights

Why joint entropy

1

Overlapping views share information.

Two views of the same wall do not tell you twice as much about it. Their observations are correlated through the shared scene parameters, and that correlation is not negligible.

2

Summing per-view entropies double-counts it.

Existing visibility-based estimators score one rendered image at a time. Adding those scores along a trajectory accumulates redundant gain and overestimates how much the trajectory is worth.

3

The chain rule fixes it — if you can afford it.

Joint entropy decomposes into conditional terms, each measuring only what a view adds beyond the ones already planned. Evaluating those terms exactly would mean retraining the radiance field at every step. JEVIS gets them for the price of a single training run.

04 · Abstract

Abstract

Active mapping requires planning observations that maximally reduce uncertainty about an unknown scene. Recent work uses visibility fields to quantify uncertainty in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), assigning higher uncertainty to unobserved regions. However, existing approaches score the entropy of a single rendered image and therefore primarily support myopic next-best-view selection. Practical deployments instead require estimating the joint entropy of multiple observations, such as views along a long-horizon trajectory or viewpoints assigned across a multi-robot system. In these settings, overlapping views observe the same region of the scene, and the correlation between their observations is not negligible.

We present Joint Entropy Estimation in 3DGS via Visibility field (JEVIS), a principled framework for computing the joint entropy of multiple synthesized views of a trained 3DGS. By leveraging a maximum-likelihood observation (MLO) approximation, JEVIS accurately estimates joint entropy across multiple views without repeated radiance field retraining. This lightweight formulation extends naturally to multi-agent active mapping. We evaluate JEVIS in long-horizon single-agent and multi-agent active mapping across diverse indoor and space-robotics environments. Results show that JEVIS more accurately captures joint uncertainty and produces higher-fidelity reconstructions than existing methods.

05 · Method

From per-view entropy to joint entropy

Three moves: decompose the objective with the chain rule, make each conditional term tractable with a maximum-likelihood observation, and realise the whole decomposition by updating the visibility field in place as candidate poses are scored.

The formulation

STEP 01

Chain rule

Maximising expected information gain over a pose sequence reduces to maximising the joint entropy of the observations, which decomposes into a sum of conditional entropies — each counting only what a view adds beyond the ones already planned.

STEP 02

MLO approximation

The not-yet-acquired observations are replaced by their most likely values under the current model — the rendered means. An intractable marginalisation collapses to a single point evaluation while cross-view dependence on θ is preserved.

STEP 03

Update the field, not the model

Score pose k against the field, absorb it with UpdateVF, then score pose k+1. We prove this returns exactly the MLO-approximated joint entropy — with zero extra retraining passes.

Joint entropy, decomposed by the chain rule. The conditional terms are what make overlap cost nothing twice — so the joint entropy is strictly smaller than the sum of per-view marginals.

The maximum-likelihood observation approximation: unknown future observations become the rendered means under the current model.

The algorithm

Require: past obs z1:t, past poses p1:t, candidate pt+1:t+L
Ensure:  joint entropy H

▷ train 3DGS — once
θ*t ← argminθ Σk=1..t ‖ f(pk; θ) − zk ‖²

▷ build the visibility field from past poses
V0 ← InitVF(θ*t)
for k = 1 to t do
    Vk ← UpdateVF(Vk−1, θ*t, pk)

▷ score the candidate trajectory
for k = t+1 to t+L do
    Hk ← GAVIS(pk; Vk−1, θ*t)
    Vk ← UpdateVF(Vk−1, θ*t, pk)

return Σk=t+1..t+L Hk

The highlighted line is the whole method.

Remove it and the field freezes at Vt for the entire candidate loop. Every pose is then scored independently, JEVIS collapses to a naive sum of per-view marginals, and overlapping viewpoints get counted twice.

Proposition (correctness).

Under the MLO approximation, this algorithm returns exactly the joint entropy that an idealised procedure would — one that retrains the 3DGS and rebuilds the visibility field at every step. The proof shows the parameters never move (θ*k−1 = θ*t), so the fields and the per-step entropies agree by induction. JEVIS therefore adds no approximation beyond MLO while eliminating L retraining passes per candidate trajectory.

Multi-agent extension

With M agents each committing to one trajectory from its own candidate pool, exhaustive search over the product space is exponential in M. But joint entropy is monotone submodular in the selected view set and the one-trajectory-per-agent constraint is a partition matroid, so sequential greedy selection carries a 1/2 approximation guarantee.

We keep one shared running visibility field and visit agents in a uniformly random permutation, resampled at every planning step so imbalanced pools never create a systematic priority bias. Each agent scores against a field that already encodes every earlier commitment — overlap across agents is discounted by exactly the same mechanism that handles overlap within a trajectory.

06 · Uncertainty

What the estimator actually sees

Per-pose uncertainty maps along one candidate trajectory. GAVIS and JEVIS start from the same partially-covering training views, so at the first pose they are identical. From the second pose onward they diverge: GAVIS keeps scoring against the fixed training set, while JEVIS folds in visibility from the preceding poses — and tracks ground-truth visibility.

Pose dist.→ GT RGB→ Pred. RGB→ GAVIS→ JEVIS (ours)→ GT visibility LowHigh uncertainty

GT Vis. denotes ground-truth mesh visibility, with bright regions indicating surfaces unseen by the training views and by preceding trajectory poses. Accurate uncertainty quantification assigns higher uncertainty to regions with lower ground-truth visibility — JEVIS uncertainty closely aligns with GT Vis. throughout each trajectory.
