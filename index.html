TL;DR

JEVIS is a principled framework for quantifying the joint information gain of multiple planned observations in 3DGS, enabling long-horizon and multi-agent active perception.

Abstract

Active mapping requires planning observations that maximally reduce uncertainty about an unknown scene. Recent methods have substantially improved uncertainty quantification in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by explicitly modeling visibility, assigning higher uncertainty to unobserved regions. However, these methods estimate the entropy of a single image observation and therefore primarily support myopic next-best-view selection. Practical deployments instead require estimating the joint entropy of multiple observations, such as views along a long-horizon trajectory or viewpoints assigned across a team of robots. In these settings, different views can observe common unseen regions of the scene, and the correlation between their observations is not negligible. To this end, we present Joint Entropy estimation in 3DGS via VISibility field (JEVIS), a theoretically grounded framework for quantifying the expected information gain of multiple planned observations in 3DGS through joint entropy estimation. By leveraging a maximum-likelihood observation (MLO) approximation, JEVIS efficiently estimates joint entropy across multiple views. We evaluate JEVIS in long-horizon single-agent and multi-agent active mapping across diverse indoor and space-robotics environments. Results show that JEVIS more accurately captures joint uncertainty and produces higher-fidelity reconstructions than existing methods.

Contributions

  1. We estimate the joint entropy of planned 3DGS observations to guide long-horizon active mapping.
  2. We extend the formulation to multi-robot planning, offering, to our knowledge, the first information-theoretic approach to long-horizon multi-agent active mapping with radiance fields.
  3. Across indoor and space environments, JEVIS improves reconstruction quality over baselines in single- and multi-agent settings.
